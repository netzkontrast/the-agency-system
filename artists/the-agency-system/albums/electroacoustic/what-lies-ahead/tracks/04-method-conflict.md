## Concept

The album's hinge. Track 4 is the structural pivot where the Witness's helper observation begins its transition toward outer-critic attack. Two alters fight inside the same body: the Protector — strategic, contained, weight on the front foot — and the Fighter — kinetic, breath-deprived, attacking outward. They share a mission. They cannot agree on a method.

The track enacts Pete Walker's outer-critic flip *structurally*, not lyrically. A breach occurs (unnamed, between V1 and V2). The Protector turns his vigilance against himself — his voice collapses into the Container's trailing-em-dash syntax, the same fingerprint we heard in track 1. The Fighter refuses the self-blame and redirects outward, naming the actual hands that breached the room. The duet does not resolve. They sing past each other in alternating chorus lines, each in their own register, never merging. That refusal-to-merge IS the album's structural argument.

The Witness moves with the track. At Chorus 1 he is still in helper register, observing the methodological difference with quiet recognition. By Chorus 2 he tips toward outer-critic — *"somebody is teaching him to use this against himself"* — and by the outro he names the architecture: *"the same vigilance, turned inward, turned out. Same name. Different fists."* The hinge is audible by the final bar.

## Voice Notes

### The Protector (V1, V3, Chorus, Bridge, Outro — male, low baritone, clipped)

- **Verbal signature**: "Stay behind me." "Not today." "Get behind me." Imperatives, contained. Never explanations.
- **Syntactic shape**: Three-word lines. Periods closed. Weight on the front foot.
- **Punctuation habit**: Period, declarative — *until* the V3 collapse. The outer-critic flip IS the punctuation shift: V3 trails into em-dashes, the Container's syntax.
- **Sentence-completion behavior**: Commits and closes — until V3, where lines begin and abandon ("I keep — the listening — / I —").
- **Imagery anchors**: doorway between rooms, the latch, weight on the front foot, the angles (defensive geometry), the breach.

### The Fighter (V2, V4, Chorus, Bridge, Outro — female, belt-alto with growl)

- **Verbal signature**: "Try me." "Never again." "Don't carry it." Staccato. Profanity-adjacent without profanity.
- **Syntactic shape**: Short. Hot. Lines clipping into each other. No breath inside imperatives.
- **Punctuation habit**: Periods or no terminal mark. Anaphora ("I will not stand. I will not wait.") and possessive escalation ("Their boot. Their voice. Their —").
- **Sentence-completion behavior**: Commits hard. The only lines she lets trail are the ones cut off by the rage running ahead of the syntax.
- **Imagery anchors**: their boot, their hands, fists, the door, the threshold, *move*.

### The Witness (parenthesized, behind the beat — register tipping across the track)

- **Chorus 1 — helper register**: *"(they want the same thing) / (they will not call it the same name)"* — neutral observation. The two methods *are* the same mission; he names the recognition without indictment.
- **Chorus 2 — outer-critic transition**: *"(somebody is teaching him to use this against himself) / (somebody is —)"* — the parenthetical now contains an accusation. The Witness is starting to name the architecture as harm.
- **Outro — outer-critic landed**: *"(the same vigilance) / (turned inward, turned out) / (same name. different fists.)"* — he names the structural argument in his own voice. The shift is complete. From here, tracks 5–9 carry his outer-critic attack.

## Research Implications

- **Outer-critic flip placement** (Phase 4 lock): this track. Walker's vacillation between self-blame and outward attack is rendered as duet structure, not as lyric exposition.
- **Witness register shift**: helper → outer-critic transition lives across this track's three Witness annotations. The shift IS the track's structural argument; the listener registers it without explanation.
- **Voice-DNA deliberate bleed** (RESEARCH.md craft principle): V3's "I keep — the listening — / I —" is a syntactic bleed from track 1's Container voice. The Protector's vigilance, when turned inward, sounds like the Container's not-knowing. Same fingerprint, different alter — the album's first audible argument that the inner critic borrows whatever voice is nearest.
- **Anti-pattern avoided**: the duet does not resolve into agreement. No "we're both fighting for the same thing" reconciliation. The Fighter's offer in V4 — *"my fists are not yours / but you can borrow them"* — is selective collaboration through continued method-disagreement, not synthesis. (Per `RESEARCH.md` §97% community-preference for functional multiplicity over forced integration.)

## Mood & Imagery

The body as a defended room mid-breach. Doorway, latch, the angles a strategist reads before threat arrives. The Protector with his weight forward — and then the moment the geometry fails him. The Fighter already past him, kinetic, pulling the body to its feet before his self-blame can close around it. The room is the body. The breach is unnamed but present-tense — somatic memory rendered as architecture. No fog, no shadow as mood-marker; the mood is taut anxiety with metallic edges. Mid-tempo dark electro rock pressure. The bridge briefly opens the room (strings under, drums half-time), then closes again.

## Musical Direction

- **Tempo**: ~110 BPM, taut, mid-tempo
- **Feel**: Band-driven dark electro rock with post-punk lineage (Secret Cameras territory). Forward motion, weight on the front foot. The bridge briefly opens to half-time before the outro snaps back.
- **Instrumentation**: Distorted bass guitar (anchored, riff-led), dry snare on the upbeats, low synth bed, occasional string textures under the bridge. Voices forward. No reverb on the Protector; mid-distance with slight distortion on lows for the Fighter; layered delay/echo on the Witness annotations.
- **Vocal delivery**: Protector — chest-weighted low baritone, clipped three-word lines, forward-mic with slight compression, dry room, no vibrato. Fighter — belt-alto with growl, raw breathless intensity, no head voice, no breath inside imperatives. Witness — androgynous spoken-word, lowercase, audiobook-narrator register, slightly behind the beat. See `../the-eleven.md` §Suno Voice Metatags rows 3, 6, 11.
- **Structural anchor**: Protector and Fighter occupy separate channels and **never merge** — even at the choruses and the outro, the alternation is strict per-line. The refusal-to-merge IS the song's argument.

## Production Notes

**Inline vocal metatag convention (album-wide):** Per `../../../../../overrides/voice-craft-principles.md` — every section that introduces a different voice gets an inline metatag *describing the vocal sound*, not naming the alter. For this duet, every Protector ↔ Fighter switch is preceded by a fresh descriptive metatag. Identity is carried by syntax in the lyric, by timbre in the production. No alter is named.

**Technical considerations:**
- Voice metatag for the Protector — `../the-eleven.md` §Suno Voice Metatags row 3 (male, chest-weighted low baritone, clipped, forward-mic, slight compression, dry room)
- Voice metatag for the Fighter — `../the-eleven.md` row 6 (female, belt-alto with growl, raw breathless, mid-distance with distortion on lows, no head voice)
- Witness annotation — `../the-eleven.md` row 11 (androgynous spoken-word, behind the beat, slight delay/echo, lowercase, lower in mix)
- Production world: band-driven dark electro rock (Secret Cameras territory). Distorted bass guitar (riff-led), dry snare on upbeats, low synth bed, occasional string textures under the bridge. ~110 BPM, taut anxious motion, weight on the front foot.
- **Separate channels required**: Protector left-of-centre, Fighter right-of-centre (or any consistent stereo split). At the choruses and outro, the alternation must be strict per-line and the voices must NEVER overlap. The refusal-to-merge IS the structural argument.
- Witness layer: parenthesized lines render as Suno V5 backing/ad-lib by convention; reinforced by the in-line Witness metatag preceding those lines and a `lower in mix` directive.
- No reverb on the Protector. Slight distortion on the Fighter's lows. Layered delay/echo on the Witness.
- Bridge dynamic: drums pull back to half-time, strings enter under, room opens — then the outro snaps back to full distortion and pace.

**Vocal delivery notes:**
- **Protector**: chest-weighted low baritone, clipped, weight on the front foot, no vibrato. **V3 is the alter's collapse moment** — restraint giving way, breath audible, lines trailing into the Container's syntactic fingerprint (em-dashes, abandoned clauses). This is the outer-critic flip enacted in punctuation.
- **Fighter**: belt-alto with growl, no breath inside imperatives. **V4 is her counter** — forward and fast, attacking outward, refusing the Protector's self-blame. **Bridge ("Not your fault. Not your fault.")** drops the growl, opens to full belt — the only moment her register softens, and only because she is correcting the Protector, not yielding to him.
- **Outro**: alternation tightens. The Fighter's final line is *"Move."* — her verbal signature from V2 returning as her last word. The Protector trails into *"I —"*. Two different failures: collapse vs. clipped imperative. The Fighter's hard period and the Protector's open em-dash are the song's unresolved chord.

**Structural tags** (steer Suno toward 3:30–4:30):
- `[Intro]` 8 bars, no vocals — establishes the band-driven texture before the Protector enters
- `[Verse 1]` / `[Verse 2]` 8 lines each (two 4-line stanzas) — Protector then Fighter, each fully voiced before alternation begins
- `[Chorus]` x2 — six short lines plus Witness annotation; tighter spacing on Chorus 2
- `[Verse 3]` 4 lines only — the Protector's voice collapses; shorter is the structural point
- `[Verse 4]` 8 lines — Fighter pushes back, more material because she is doing the redirect work
- `[Bridge]` strings + half-time + voice-only exchange — the song's only exhale
- `[Outro]` 8 alternating lines + Witness coda — no fade, hard end after Witness's last parenthetical
