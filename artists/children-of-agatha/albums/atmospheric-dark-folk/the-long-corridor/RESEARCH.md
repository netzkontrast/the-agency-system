# The Long Corridor — Research & Source Documentation

This document provides citations and background research for the album *The Long Corridor* by Children of Agatha. The album is inspired by the real St. Agatha Krankenhaus in Köln and the experience of patients undergoing psychosomatic and psychiatric treatment there. All factual claims about the institution, its programs, and psychosomatic medicine are documented here.

**Album type**: Inspired By Real Events — not a first-person account of named patients. The album draws on the institutional context and the general experience of inpatient care at St. Agatha, not on identifiable individual cases.

**Sensitivity**: Patient privacy is paramount. No identifiable patient information is used or sought. Research focuses on the institution, its publicly documented programs, and the clinical context of psychosomatic and psychiatric care.

## Institution

### Full Official Name (since 2023)

**Cellitinnen-Marienborn St. Agatha Fachklinik für Seelische Gesundheit**

Since 2023 the institution operates exclusively as a specialist mental health clinic — psychiatry, psychosomatic medicine, and psychotherapy. General medicine and surgery were shed to concentrate on mental health care. The colloquial name "St. Agatha Krankenhaus" persists locally and in historical usage.

### Conditions Treated

Depression, bipolar disorder, anxiety disorders, PTSD, addiction, schizophrenic psychoses, OCD, eating disorders (anorexia, bulimia, binge eating), somatoform and neurotic disorders, geriatric psychiatry.

### Kulturkapelle and Community Integration

The former convent chapel was repurposed as the **Kulturkapelle** — a public art exhibition space, free admission, open to the community. This physical space bridges the patient and public spheres within the institution's walls. The hospital is also one of 22 stations on the **Niehler Kulturpfad**, a cultural walking trail through the neighbourhood.

These facts are significant for the album concept: St. Agatha does not seal itself from the outside world. It reaches toward it through art.

## Music and Creative Therapies

Music therapy has been practiced at St. Agatha for **over 40 years** (documented from the official website). The following is taken directly from the clinic's own published descriptions:

### Musiktherapie (Music Therapy)

The official website describes music therapy at St. Agatha as:

> "erlebnisorientiert und handlungsorientiert" (experience-oriented and action-oriented)

Group music sessions are framed as:

> "ein geschützter Raum für Begegnung und Spiel" — **a protected space for encounter and play**

The stated aims: developing emotional capacity, interpersonal awareness, and non-verbal communication. Music therapy is offered as a group modality, making collective music-making the therapeutic form — not individual performance.

This is the direct institutional precedent for the album's premise: patients at St. Agatha have been making music together, in a protected collective space, for over four decades.

### Kunsttherapie (Art Therapy)

Also offered for 40+ years. Uses the creative process to access feelings and memories that resist verbal articulation. Direct parallel to the music therapy rationale.

### Additional Therapeutic Modalities

- **Tanztherapie** (dance therapy) — body-focused, group or individual
- **Ergotherapie** (occupational therapy)
- **Sport- und Bewegungstherapie** — including sessions along the Rhine
- Evidence-based psychotherapy throughout all wards

## Saint Agatha — Patron and Symbol

**Agatha of Sicily** (c. 225–c. 251 CE, Catania) — Christian martyr, died under Emperor Decius. Her iconographic attribute — breasts on a platter — grounds her patronage of breast diseases and nursing care, making her a natural saint for nursing-order hospitals.

**Why the Cellitinnen chose her**: In 1900, a fire broke out at the Cellitinnen's care house at Marienborn near Zülpich. The sisters prayed to St. Agatha; the fire was contained without major loss. This was taken as her intercession, cementing her as patron of the Cologne Cellitinnen's institutions. When their Niehl hospital opened on **5 February 1905** — her feast day — the dedication was made concrete.

**For the album**: St. Agatha endured bodily mutilation and survived it; she is a figure of bodily suffering and endurance. In a hospital for psychosomatic illness — where the body speaks what the mind suppresses — her patronage carries an unintended resonance the album can draw on without making theological claims.
