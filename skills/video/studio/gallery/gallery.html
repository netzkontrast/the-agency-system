Pick a style

Every video is rendered from code with Claude Opus 5.5. Choose the look, then approve the storyboard.

01

Watercolor character

Hand-painted brushstrokes and boiling ink lines. A character acts out a small story, like a picture book that moves.

Best for: stories, music videos, anything with a journey.

Reference: "I'm Upping My P(doom)" music video

02

Clean kinetic explainer

White page, one accent color, giant word-by-word type with one italic word, a dithered mascot, product UI that assembles.

Best for: a product or idea in 15-30 seconds.

Reference: a product explainer for a workflow tool

03

Image-to-game cinematic

An image model makes the art, Opus turns it into a real animated (and playable) game. Opens on a "screenshot" that comes alive.

Best for: "the model built a game from my images", starring a real person.

Reference: a Mortal Kombat-style fight game (ours)

04

Dark premium explainer

Near-black, one warm gold accent, a glowing hero object, cinematic light. Short, big type, one idea.

Best for: serious AI and B2B concepts.

Reference: an AI infrastructure concept video (fixed: shorter, bigger, higher contrast)

05

Editorial HUD film

Photoreal fashion-campaign footage of one character (made with an image/video model), with a code-drawn HUD: data panels, tracking brackets, a countdown, a news ticker, giant kinetic words.

Best for: art pieces, brand films, a statement about AI and culture.

Reference: an Opus 5.5 film with Midjourney and a moodboard

06

Broadcast title sequence

Beat-locked typography like a sports-season opener: a color world per chapter, split-flap letters, marquee outlines, one signature visual each, HUD with BPM and bar count.

Best for: lineups and comparisons, a launch with several features, a season or event opener.

Reference: "THE FRONTIER" (Opus 5.5, 15 minutes)

07

Cinematic animated short

A wordless 1-3 minute story with a real emotional arc: one relationship, one repeated gesture, years passing, a payoff. Letterboxed, soft stylized 3D, moonlit palette.

Best for: stories that make people feel something; a brand's "why"; a tribute.

Reference: "Goodnight, Keeper"

08

Kinetic lyric video

The words are the visuals: every sung line becomes its own typographic scene with a visual metaphor. Black + one hot accent, a paper-white "technical" world for contrast.

Best for: music videos without characters, songs with strong lines.

Reference: a P(doom) lyric video

09

Painterly glow music video

Moving oil-paint: swirling Van Gogh brushstrokes, deep night blues, one glowing ember-lit figure through symbolic scenes. Dreamlike, emotional.

Best for: turning a song (Suno or yours) into an emotional clip.

Reference: a full Suno-style song turned into a clip

10

Manifesto film

A serious idea in 60-90 s: a hard fact, a true story, the turn, the vision as a numbered list, a callback. Black, paper and one signal yellow; photos turned into line art; terminals as proof.

Best for: announcing a company, an initiative, a thesis. B2B and AI.

Reference: Thom Wolf / Hugging Face "Open Alignment"

11

3D teaching song

A song that teaches: each lyric line is demonstrated in a 3D world of the subject, the sung word lights up orange, a recurring chart shows progress.

Best for: turning a book, course, paper or product into a song people rewatch.

Reference: "Chasing the Roofline"

12

Monochrome launch film

Pure black and white, hard section inversions: liquid chrome and dithered matter in the black world, quant charts with live ticking numbers in the white world, icon-scatter storytelling, one gold prestige moment.

Best for: fintech, crypto and premium dev-tool launches; brands with weak or undecided colors.

Reference: "Frontier Traders" (Opus 5.5, 20 minutes)

13

Crayon storybook

A child's picture book in motion: wobbly blue ballpoint contour, crayon-scribble fills on cream paper, a handwritten caption strip as the timeline, squash-and-stretch critters with one big eye. Imperfection is the aesthetic, so moving characters never break.

Best for: history and science explainers, "X in under a minute", kids' subjects, family films, characters that move a lot.

Reference: "The history of dinosaurs" (Sonnet 5.5, square)

14

Paper cut-out

Flat cut and torn paper layers with real drop shadows between them, hinge-and-pivot puppet motion, construction-paper colors, fiber fringe on torn edges. Variants: lightbox glow, pop-up book.

Best for: songs, histories, warm brand stories.

Previews: THE PAPER BOAT, rendered by us

15

Pixel art film

A hard pixel grid at tiny logical resolution, per-scene limited palettes, Bayer-dithered skies, sprite characters that snap between frames. Variants: lofi loop, 16-bit RPG, HD-2D, demoscene.

Best for: nostalgia stories, game-flavored product spots, lofi loops.

Previews: THE PAPER BOAT, rendered by us

16

Hand-drawn sketchbook

Pen, pencil and charcoal lines that draw themselves on paper grain, hatching for tone, construction lines left visible, handwritten margin notes, one accent wash.

Best for: intimate stories, explainers that build up, "the film draws itself".

Previews: THE PAPER BOAT, rendered by us

17

Glossy chrome showreel

Studio-lit liquid-metal type and objects on bold poster fields, beat-locked vignettes, hard cuts, extreme easing, persistent HUD with BPM and bar count.

Best for: launches, brand stings, motion-design flexes.

Previews: THE PAPER BOAT, rendered by us

18

ASCII / CRT terminal

Everything built from monospace characters on a phosphor tube: scenes as character-density fields, scanlines, prompts and log lines as narration, one red alert line allowed.

Best for: dev-tool launches, hacker stories, anything for a technical audience.

Previews: THE PAPER BOAT, rendered by us

19

Ink wash (sumi-e)

Loaded brush on rice paper, five ink dilutions, bleed halos and flying-white dry strokes, vast negative space, one cinnabar seal stamp. Strokes draw themselves.

Best for: contemplative, heritage and reflective pieces; calm authority.

Previews: THE PAPER BOAT, rendered by us

20

Animator's pencil test

An animator's desk test: red clean pencil over blue construction, onion skins, a frame counter on twos and ones. The character performs, the film freezes to dissect the motion with principle labels and a spacing chart, then an APPROVED stamp slams in.

Best for: showing craft and process, character reveals, behind-the-scenes halves of character films.

Previews: THE PAPER BOAT, rendered by us

21

Riso pop

A three-ink risograph world: yellow, fluorescent pink and blue halftone screens overprinting on cream paper, slight misregistration, flat mid-century figures, a mascot protagonist, one satirical gag per line, typewriter captions with strikethroughs.

Best for: satire, internet-culture music videos, memes, AI commentary.

Previews: THE PAPER BOAT, rendered by us
