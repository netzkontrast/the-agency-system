# Formats and devices (orthogonal to style)

Recurring creative devices from the top-performing code-rendered videos (mined from a 1,401-video corpus, Sep 2026). A style says how frames look; these say what the piece IS. Offer the fitting one at the pitch.

## Formats

- **The interactive artifact IS the video.** Build a real playable toy/lab/game, then film it being used (cursor in frame, things reacting). Evidence: a draggable black-hole lens lab and a playable Splatoon clone both spread far. Works because viewers know they could touch it too; end the caption with the link. Our angle: we already build deterministic film.html pages; making one interactive first and filming it second is a small step.
- **Production receipts.** State in the caption: brief length, hours, model, tokens, cost, and post the exact brief as the first reply. Turns a nice video into a reproducible flex and drives bookmarks (the top showreels all did this). Pairs with our honesty rule: the numbers must be measured, not vibes. Capture them during the build, not after.
- **Model-vs-model duel.** The same brief run on two models, split-screen with labels. A format people reflexively share to argue in the replies (a case that spread widely). Cheap for us: our briefs are files; running one twice is free.
- **The film draws itself.** The making-of is the payoff: strokes arrive in a hand's order, the press does its make-ready, the scene assembles. Strongest with sketch/ink/crayon/paper styles. Mechanic: marks as data, replayed over a cached composite (see the style files).
- **Genre parody.** A famous launch grammar applied verbatim, deadpan, to an absurd subject: the reveal music, the slow orbit, the "Introducing", the spec callouts, the price slam, for a virus or a baguette. Evidence: a launch parody for a virus and one for a baguette both spread. Works because everyone knows the grammar and the gap between it and the subject is the joke; never wink at the camera. Mimic the grammar, not the brand: no real logos or marks of the company being parodied.
- **Throughline object.** One tiny element survives every cut (a dot, a cursor, a coin, a paper plane) and carries the eye from scene to scene, then ends exactly where it began so the video loops seamlessly. It is the shared-element handoff (`motion-kit.md`) made into the whole structure.
- **Remake mode.** A frame-locked 1:1 remake of a reference launch video for your own brand: same shots, same timings, same music hits, your product. Post it split-screen (original left, remake right). Credit the original by name in frame or caption, and no fake co-marks (their logo beside yours implies a partnership that doesn't exist). Lock timings from the reference's contact sheet (`scripts/contact-sheet.sh`) and cut list before building.
- **One continuous zoom, no cuts.** Macro-to-micro or micro-to-cosmos in a single shot (glass → atoms; a country → the observable universe). We proved it in-house with an infinite-zoom film for a whiteboard product. The discipline: plan the scale ladder first, each decade of zoom needs its own authored content.

## Levers

- **Sincere first-person AI stories out-earn spec demos** in the story cluster (top cases were "Claude reflects on its life" and "collecting human warmth"). A subject-matter lever to OFFER, never to impose (no unasked themes).
- **Scene-synthesized sound:** the scene's physics is the score — every collision plays a note, materials have voices. Beyond cutting to a track; needs the code SFX/music engine.
- **Data guard:** any on-screen number must come from a data slot that refuses invented values (one popular case made the guard itself the story). Extends our "claims literally true" rule to figures.
- **Length exception:** the 30-45 s feed rule stands, but reviewed educational explainers had a ~102 s median and still performed. A teaching piece may run long; a launch piece may not.
