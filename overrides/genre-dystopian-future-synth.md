# Dystopian Future Synth — Genre Profile

## 0. How to use this override

This document defines what "Dystopian Future Synth" means as a working umbrella for The Agency System albums. It supersedes the thinner plugin-side genre README at `genres/dystopian-future-synth/README.md` (which is plugin-cache-ephemeral and exists primarily for MCP-side validation).

When in conflict with the plugin-cache copy, **this override is canonical**.

For distribution / streaming-platform tags: do not use "dystopian-future-synth" as the surface tag — it is not journalism-recognized. Surface-tag with the closest journalism-recognized tags per §1: darksynth, darkwave, post-punk, industrial, EBM (pick per-track based on which the specific track honestly inhabits).

## 1. Term provenance

**Status of the term: artist-coined umbrella, not a journalism-recognized scene.**

"Dystopian Future Synth" does not appear as a defined entry on the major reference sites that catalogue electronic-music microgenres. Wikipedia's Synthwave article catalogues Darksynth, Outrun, Dreamwave, and Scifiwave as named subgenres but does not list "Dystopian Future Synth" [1]. Wikipedia's Dark Wave article catalogues ethereal wave, neoclassical dark wave, cold wave, and dark cabaret as subgenres, again with no entry [2]. Bandcamp's tag search for "futuresynth" returns zero releases as of this research [3]; Last.fm's `futuresynth` tag page exists but the wiki section is unpopulated [4].

What *does* exist on the same conceptual ground:

- **Darksynth** — recognized subgenre with extensive coverage (Rate Your Music, Wikipedia, Bandcamp, label catalogues including Blood Music, Federal Prisoner, Lazerdiscs; dedicated journalism via Electrozombies, Invisible Oranges, Kerrang!) [5][6][7]
- **Cyberpunk synthwave** — used descriptively on Last.fm's `cyberpunk` tag and in journalism around Cyberpunk 2077 [8][9]
- **Dark synth-pop / dark synthpop** — used on platform genre charts (Chosic, volt.fm) for artists fusing synth-pop structure with darkwave atmosphere [10]
- **Futuresynth** — present as a Last.fm tag with a few thousand listeners on the top-tagged artists (Lyde, Dawnrazer, Beasuce), but undefined as a wiki entry and absent from Bandcamp [4][3]

**Synthesis.** "Dystopian Future Synth" functions in this project as an artist-coined umbrella by The Agency System / Michael Schimmer, gathering territory that journalism splits across darksynth, post-2018 darkwave revival, post-industrial, and dystopian-themed synth-pop. Treat it as a working frame for production decisions, not as a label that listeners will recognize from a Bandcamp tag.

**Recommended distribution tagging:**
- Primary tag: `darksynth` for instrumental cyberpunk-coded material; `darkwave` for vocal-led material
- Plural tags: add `post-punk`, `industrial`, `synthwave`, `EBM` where the specific track honestly inhabits those traditions
- Do NOT tag releases with "dystopian-future-synth" as surface metadata — distributors and aggregators won't index it; listeners won't search for it

## 2. Adjacent and overlapping genres — differentiation table

| Adjacent genre | Defining sonic signature | What Dystopian Future Synth inherits | What differentiates |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Darksynth** | Distorted analog/saturated bass arpeggios, minor-key motifs, horror-film synth motifs, four-on-the-floor or breakbeat drums, predominantly instrumental [6][11] | Saturated bass, minor keys, cinematic motifs, mid-tempo drive | Darksynth is overwhelmingly instrumental and horror/occult-coded (Perturbator, Carpenter Brut, GosT); Dystopian Future Synth here includes vocal-led material and a wider thematic range (political/technological dread, survivor-narrative) |
| **Industrial (post-2015)** | Distorted percussion, sampled mechanical noise, guitar layering, harsh-vocal aggression (3TEETH, HEALTH, Author & Punisher) [12][13][14] | Distortion, dystopian lyric register, machine-as-instrument | Industrial leans into rhythmic abrasion and (often) guitars; Dystopian Future Synth keeps synthesizers central, reserves abrasion for accents rather than whole arrangements |
| **Darkwave (post-2010 revival)** | Mid-tempo synth-driven post-punk with baritone or icy vocals, drum-machine pulse, often guitar accents (Drab Majesty, Twin Tribes, Actors, She Past Away) [2][15][16][17] | Baritone vocal tradition, drum-machine pulse, gothic atmosphere | Darkwave rooted in post-punk songform and 1980s gothic lineage; Dystopian Future Synth pushes toward more aggressive synthesis and overt sci-fi/technological dread |
| **Coldwave** | Minimal analog synthesis, militaristic rhythm, detached vocals; French/Belgian/Polish lineage; modern revivalists Molchat Doma, Lebanon Hanover [18][19][20] | Detached vocal delivery, minimalist arrangements at quieter moments, analog synth palette | Coldwave is quieter and more austere; Dystopian Future Synth tolerates wider dynamic range and bigger production gestures |
| **Post-punk (baritone lineage)** | Joy Division/Bauhaus-descended low vocal delivery, angular guitar, locked-in bass [21] | Baritone vocal register, sparse arrangements at verse, repetitive drive | Post-punk centers guitar and rhythm section; Dystopian Future Synth centers synthesis, treats guitar as accent |
| **Witch house** | Pitched-down vocals, trap-style drum loops, occult symbols, lo-fi haze (Salem, oOoOO) [22] | Slowed-down sections, processed vocal stacks | Witch house is largely vocal-sample-based and explicitly occult; Dystopian Future Synth keeps melodic vocal lines and sci-fi/political/survivor rather than occult themes |
| **Cyberpunk synthwave** | Sci-fi narrative framing, neon-noir mood, more melodic and less aggressive than darksynth [8][23] | Sci-fi narrative frame, neon-noir mood | Cyberpunk synthwave often stays bright and consonant within its dystopian frame; Dystopian Future Synth allows more dissonance and abrasion |
| **EBM revival (post-2015)** | Sequenced bass, four-on-the-floor, shouted/processed vocals, dancefloor functionality (Boy Harsher, Kontravoid, post-2020 Sextile) [24][25][26][27] | Sequenced bass, dancefloor tempo on club tracks | EBM purpose-built for the dancefloor; Dystopian Future Synth treats dancefloor function as one mode among several |
| **Dystopian synth-pop / dark synthpop** | Synth-pop structure (verse/chorus/hook) with dark lyrical content (Light Asylum, Mareux, Molchat Doma) [10][28] | Verse-chorus structure, hook-led writing, dark lyric register | This overlaps almost entirely with the vocal half of Dystopian Future Synth; the difference is mainly that dark synthpop stays closer to pop song forms while Dystopian Future Synth permits longer instrumental passages and more cinematic structure |

## 5. Sonic characteristics

Concrete techniques actually used by artists in this territory:

- **Saturated, sustained bass.** Reese and FM basses processed with saturation, bitcrushing, and tape/console emulation — documented standard practice in darksynth production tutorials and in Perturbator/Carpenter Brut-tier work [11]. The bass is usually the loudest sustained element in the mix.
- **Analog-style polysynth pads with movement.** Juno-, Prophet-, and Oberheim-inspired poly tones; slow attack, deliberate filter sweeps. Used as harmonic pads behind sequenced lines [11][44].
- **Drum machine kits with cinematic processing.** LinnDrum, TR-707, TR-909, DMX, 808 (and modern emulations), with gated-reverb snares, large tom fills, stop-time impact hits [11][44]. Programming references 1980s rather than reproducing it.
- **Sequenced sixteenth-note synth lines.** Darksynth-derived bass arpeggios in minor keys with detuned-saw or dual-oscillator timbres, often run through chorus and saturation [11].
- **Vocal processing is sparse and deliberate.** When vocals appear, they sit in one of four documented modes:
  - Clean baritone (Drab Majesty's Andrew Clinco, Twin Tribes' Luis Navarro, Lebanon Hanover's William Maybelline) [15][36][37]
  - Whispered or icy female delivery (Boy Harsher's Jae Matthews, Selofan's Joanna Pavlidou) [24][43]
  - Spoken or shouted EBM-style (Kontravoid, Sextile) [27][26]
  - Vocoder/processed (cinematic accent in darksynth) [45]
  Heavy vocal stacking is rare; verbed and delayed mono leads are common.
- **Drumless or drum-sparse interludes.** Drab Majesty, Lebanon Hanover, and Perturbator's *Lustful Sacraments* all use extended pad-driven sections where the rhythm element drops out [15][29].
- **Guitar as texture, not lead.** When guitar appears (Actors, Twin Tribes), it functions as chorused or flanged texture more often than as melodic lead instrument [17][36].
- **Reference instruments artists actually name in interviews:** Twin Tribes cite the Roland JX-3P and Korg Poly-800 [36]. Boy Harsher uses Moog Mother-32, DFAM, Subharmonicon [25]. Production gear discussions appear on Elektronauts forums and tutorial pieces — primarily Juno polysynths, FM/wavetable layers for bite, analog drum-machine emulations [44].

## 6. Lyrical themes and conventions

Documented themes from this territory's vocal-led artists:

- **Addiction, dissatisfaction, and self-destruction.** Perturbator described *Lustful Sacraments* as being about "bad habits, dissatisfaction, and addiction," structured like "an episodic midnight journey through the ups and downs of debauchery in some dark club" [29][30].
- **Technological dread / hyper-consumerism critique.** 3TEETH's *EndEx* is "full of contempt for concepts like hyper-consumerism and tech being too prevalent in our lives" [12][13]. Drab Majesty's *Modern Mirror* unfolds "as a statement on the negative influence of technology on human personality and identity" [15].
- **Occult and undead imagery.** Twin Tribes' *Shadows* covers "the undead, and the occult and parallel universes" [36]; darksynth more broadly uses "demons, zombies, vampires" as documented horror-coded subject matter [6][9].
- **Soviet-coded post-Soviet melancholy.** Molchat Doma's lyrics (in Russian) center on architectural and existential bleakness — Brutalist apartment blocks, isolation, dissatisfaction with public life [19][20].
- **Cinematic narrative concepts.** Boy Harsher's *The Runner* was written explicitly as a horror-film score; the album functions as a soundtrack [25].
- **Detachment and emotional flatness as deliberate stance.** Coldwave's "detached, emotionless vocal delivery" tradition continues into modern darkwave/coldwave practice (Lebanon Hanover, She Past Away, Mareux) [18][16][28].
- **Romantic-gothic loss and reflection.** Twin Tribes' *Ceremony* "explored love and loss" [36]; Mareux is built on Cure-descended melancholy [28].

**Conventions that hold across the territory (synthesis-marked):**
- Lyrics tend to be short, repeating, and image-led rather than narrative — single concrete images returned to throughout the track rather than verse-by-verse storytelling
- First-person address common; second-person ("you") common; third-person narrative rare
- Direct political naming is rare; political/technological dread communicated through atmosphere and metaphor more often than slogan
- AI-clichés to avoid: "neon glow / city lights / ones and zeros / through the wires / circuit board" — tells of weak imitation

## 7. Mastering and mix conventions

Documented streaming-platform targets and territory's working practice:

- **Spotify normalises to roughly -14 LUFS; Apple Music to roughly -16 LUFS; Deezer to roughly -15 LUFS** [46][47]. Loudness normalisation does not mandate a master LUFS — masters louder will be turned down at delivery; masters quieter will not always be turned up.
- **Safe-zone master target: -14 to -16 LUFS integrated, True Peak ceiling at -1 dBTP** [46][47]. For tracks pushed louder (-8 to -10 LUFS), use -2.0 dBTP to allow codec headroom.
- **Dynamic range expectation.** Industrial-leaning material (HEALTH, 3TEETH, Author & Punisher) tolerates heavier limiting and lower DR. Darkwave/coldwave material (Drab Majesty, Lebanon Hanover, Molchat Doma) benefits from preserving DR for the verse-chorus dynamic. Treat each track individually rather than applying one preset album-wide.
- **Tonal balance.** Saturated bass under 100 Hz is central to the darksynth-derived palette — keep sub controlled and don't over-EQ the 60-100 Hz region where bass synth fundamentals sit [11][48]. The 200-400 Hz range tends to be a buildup zone in synth-heavy mixes and benefits from a gentle master-bus dip.
- **Saturation as character, not loudness.** Tape, console, and analog-modeled saturators are the territory's standard glue; pure brick-wall limiting tends to flatten the cinematic accents (drum impact hits, gated-reverb snare bursts) [11].
- **Reverb and delay as compositional, not glue.** Gated reverb on snares, long plate verbs on lead synths, tempo-synced delays on vocal accents — all documented standard [11][44]. Master-bus reverb is rare.

**For The Agency System's *Lass mich, lass mich atmen* album specifically:** see `overrides/mastering-presets.yaml` for the per-genre LUFS targets. Recommend album-mode unified mastering at -13 LUFS due to per-track density spread (ambient closers at -18 LUFS, industrial peaks at -10 LUFS within track would otherwise create 8 LU shuffle-playlist incoherence).

## 8. Live performance landscape

- **Boy Harsher documented live practice:** stage filled with smoke and strobes, vocalist Jae Matthews delivering lines while kneeling, live drum performance against backing tracks for ritualistic effect [49][50]. Hardware setup documented (Mother-32, DFAM, Subharmonicon) [25].
- **Darksynth live practice.** Perturbator and Carpenter Brut perform with hardware/laptop setups against heavy visual programming; Carpenter Brut tours with a live band, increasingly bringing in the 1990s-rock palette of *Leather Terror* [31][32]. Both are heavy festival draws in Europe.
- **Festival infrastructure exists.** Wave-Gotik-Treffen (Leipzig, annual since 1992; ~20,000 attendees) is the largest dedicated festival, covering post-punk, gothic rock, darkwave, synth-pop, minimal, and industrial across multiple venues [51][2]. Cruel World (Pasadena, USA) covers the new wave / post-punk / goth / alternative side, with major heritage acts and select modern darkwave on lineup (Ministry, Gary Numan, Tones on Tail in 2024) [52]. Cold Waves (Chicago) covers industrial. Roadburn (Tilburg) hosts crossover acts including Boy Harsher [49].
- **Club-circuit infrastructure.** Klub Nocturno (LA), Substance Festival (LA), DSF (NY/LA), dedicated darkwave/post-punk DJ nights worldwide host mid-tier touring circuit [50].
- **Synthesis.** Live performance for vocal-led acts typically uses pre-recorded sequenced elements alongside small amount of live performance (vocals always, sometimes one drum machine, sometimes one synth). Pure laptop-and-vocalist setups common; full-band setups (Actors, Twin Tribes, 3TEETH) exist but minority. Visual programming — fog, strobes, projected typography or video — near-universal and is part of how the territory differentiates from generic indie performance.

## 9. Critical reception

- **Perturbator's *Lustful Sacraments*** received largely positive but split critical reception. Reviews noted the deliberate pivot: "Perturbator left behind the neon-tinged nostalgia of retrowave in favor of the oppressive and brooding sounds of industrial, post-punk and hard techno" with "a heavier focus on gothic, post-punk undertones" [29][30]. Angry Metal Guy, Heavy Music HQ, Clash, Distorted Sound, and Sonic Perspectives all reviewed; consensus described it as "remarkably cohesive" while some legacy fans called it "an underwhelming experience" relative to earlier retrowave material [29][30].
- **HEALTH's *Rat Wars*** was described as "more focused with their explorations between the fringes of industrial, metal and the blackest reaches of synthwave hitting with more consistent power" [14]. Album debuted #1 on Apple's Alternative Albums Chart and reached top 10 of Spotify's Albums Chart [14].
- **3TEETH's *EndEx*** described in Metal Sucks as "industrial metal carnage crafted for a dystopian world," continuing what one reviewer called "the Fear Factory tradition as an industrial metal band preoccupied with how technological advancements adversely affect our lives" [12][13].
- **Drab Majesty's *Modern Mirror*** scored Metacritic 80 across six reviews; PopMatters called it the project's "darkwave peak" [15][35].
- **Sextile's *Push*** received mixed-to-positive reviews; AllMusic's Heather Phares wrote it "proves that punk and electronic music can still sound dangerous and exciting when they're thrown together" [26][42].
- **Kontravoid** described by industrial outlet I Die: You Die as "the endboss of modern electronic darkwave" [27].
- **General journalistic framing.** Coverage in Post-Punk.com, Electrozombies, ReGen Magazine, Brooklyn Vegan, I Die: You Die, and CVLT Nation provides the territory's working press infrastructure. Pitchfork covers select releases (Sextile's *Push*, Molchat Doma) but does not maintain a dedicated darkwave or darksynth beat; reference-site aggregation (Album of the Year, Rate Your Music) is where critical consensus most clearly lives [53].

## 10. Audience demographics and adjacent communities

- **Gen Z is the primary growth audience.** TikTok #goth content has accumulated over 13 billion views; classic catalogue (Cure, Bauhaus, Sisters of Mercy) reports unprecedented streaming numbers among 18-24 year olds; new-act activity (Twin Tribes, Actors, Vision Video, Then Comes Silence, Bootblacks) shows tours "packed with young people" [54][55]. The 2022 Netflix release of *Wednesday* is repeatedly cited as a 2023-2024 accelerant for goth-adjacent aesthetics [54].
- **Geographic strength.** Eastern Europe was the early adopter region for Mareux's viral "The Perfect Girl" before global TikTok spread [28]. Active scenes exist across the US (LA, Vancouver, Brownsville TX, NY, Chicago, Denver), Western and Central Europe (UK, France, Germany, Greece, Italy), Turkey (She Past Away as origin point), Belarus/Russia (Molchat Doma), and Latin America.
- **Cross-platform behavior.** Bandcamp remains the territory's primary direct-to-fan platform (label catalogues for Artoffact, Sacred Bones, Blood Music, Fabrika, Dais all visible there). YouTube curator channels (NewRetroWave, Synth Diet) continue to drive the synthwave/darksynth side. TikTok drives the darkwave-vocal side. Last.fm tagging functions as the most honest read on what listeners group together (synthwave and darkwave tags both show millions; "futuresynth" remains a niche tag with thousands).
- **Adjacent communities the audience overlaps with:** post-punk revival listeners (Interpol, The Soft Moon), shoegaze revival (Slowdive, DIIV listeners increasingly cross over), industrial-metal listeners (Nine Inch Nails, Author & Punisher), neoperreo and Latin-coded goth scenes (LA's documented Gothicumbia scene) [55], cyberpunk-fiction readers, vintage-synth gear communities, dungeon-synth / dark-ambient communities sharing Bandcamp shelf space [56].
